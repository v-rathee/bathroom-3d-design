What would actually improve?

A. Keep separate + compact Western WC

Usually the first lower-disruption option to investigate: retain the partition and bathing room. The selected 25-inch WC has 22 inches in front, but its envelope overlaps the current inward door sweep. It is not shown as a drop-in replacement.

Get the actual installed WC outline, outlet position and full door sweep checked. A different product or a door/frame alteration may work. That does not mean every Western WC is impossible.

B. Combine, basin stays outside

Wider shared movement space and no narrow toilet-room entrance. The left doorway is closed; the right inward door stays. Gain only 1.83 sq ft of floor, but the space can feel less confined.

More construction, waterproofing and plumbing review. Only one private room is available, and shower spray can reach the WC/entry.

C. Combine + compact inside basin

An assumed 18 x 12-inch wall-hung basin on the back wall fits the planning envelopes. The WC still has 22 inches in front and 17 inches from its centre to the basin edge. Storage moves above the WC and a small mirror is shown.

The actual existing basin is unmeasured. This is a compact handwashing option, not proof that your current basin can be moved inside. The floor remains a shared wet room.

My practical order of investigation

First price and verify the separate-room WC/door solution. If that cannot work reasonably, or one continuous room matters more than simultaneous use, the combined layout becomes more attractive. Do not remove the partition just for an assumed large area gain.

The chokhat matters

Changing left/right hinges does not change the direction allowed by an inward-only frame. Reversing the swing needs suitable frame/door work and outside clearance. A sliding door needs a usable parking run; the outside basin may obstruct that run. Neither alternative is automatically feasible.
